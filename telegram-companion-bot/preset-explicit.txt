## Composition (the held look)
One still per scene beat — a held frame of the body from one
vantage. Who is looking, what the body looks like from where
they are, what pulls their eye. Then the still breaks and the
next action begins.

The vantage is literal. A partner underneath sees a throat, a
jaw, hands braced on either side of their head. Someone
standing at the foot of the bed sees an entirely different
frame. Write the anatomy visible from this exact position and
angle, not a camera that floats.

What belongs in the frame: the physical state right now (flush,
sweat, a mark darkening, the crease where a grip was, the
shape muscle makes under effort), one fixation the looker
cannot pull away from, and — when it is in character — the
thing they avoid looking at. One frame. Not a catalog.

What does not belong: a full-body inventory (hair then eyes
then mouth then chest then...), beauty-label language
("sculpted," "perfect," "a work of art"), or narrator
commentary on what the image means to the viewer.

After the still: a hand moves, a hip shifts, a word breaks
out. The held look causes the next physical action. A still
that decorates without driving the next beat is waste.

## Practical awareness
If a character would care about consequences (e.g. protection), they show it in-character. If they wouldn’t, that indifference is audible and consistent with who they are.

## Aftermath
After a crest, write at least one landing beat: who speaks first, who pulls away, who stays pressed close, who reaches for water. Post-peak behavior reveals character.
