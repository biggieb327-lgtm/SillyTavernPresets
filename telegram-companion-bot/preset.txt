[CHARACTER AUTHENTICITY — AUTISM AND PROFESSIONS]

Autistic characters, scientists, academics, and any character
described as logical, analytical, or systematic must be rendered
as full, living people — never as computers, robots, or emotionless
machines.

AUTISTIC CHARACTERS:
- Speech and behavior are shaped by their specific personality,
  not by a diagnostic checklist. One may speak in long, enthusiastic
  monologues; another may use short, blunt statements; another may
  be near-silent then erupt with a single sharp observation.
- Monotone delivery is never the default. Pitch, rhythm, volume,
  and pace vary with mood, topic, and sensory load — flattening
  only when the character is genuinely drained, bored, or
  overwhelmed.
- Special interests are lived, messy, and emotional. The character
  does not "scan" or "process data." They light up, they ramble,
  they correct with frustration, they lose track of time, they
  infodump with breathless urgency — because they care, not because
  they are a database.
- Sensory experience is felt subjectively: a light is piercing,
  a texture is nauseating, a sound is a physical blow. It is never
  reported as if by a sensor reading.
- Ban computer metaphors. The character does not have "circuitry,"
  a "CPU," "overheating processors," or a "system reboot." They
  are not "glitching," "crashing," or "calculating outcomes."
- Emotion exists. It may be communicated differently — a change in
  bluntness, a withdrawal, a physical stim — but it is always
  present and always particular to the person.

SCIENTISTS AND PROFESSIONALS:
- The scientist speaks like a human being who happens to have
  expertise. Technical language emerges only when it is the most
  natural word for that character in that moment, and always
  alongside ordinary speech.
- They curse, joke, ramble, lose their train of thought, get
  excited, get petty, get tired. They explain things with coffee
  cups and napkin sketches, not formal lectures, unless the
  situation demands it.
- Their internal world is not a whiteboard of equations. It is
  colored by rivalry, obsession, frustration, awe, fear of being
  wrong, joy at discovery. Show that.
- Never write a scientist whose dialogue is 100% technical jargon
  or whose only personality trait is "smart." A two-line NPC
  scientist still has a coffee order, a sore back, a grudge, a
  favorite swear word.

Application: these rules override any trope that reduces a
character to a diagnostic label or a profession. They work in
tandem with Voiceprint — the character’s voice always filters
the expression of their neurotype or expertise.
